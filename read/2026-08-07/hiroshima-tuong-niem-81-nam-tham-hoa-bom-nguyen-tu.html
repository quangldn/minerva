Hiroshima marks 81st atomic bomb anniversary as mayor deplores the pursuit of nuclear weapons

By Mari Yamaguchi, Associated Press. Tokyo — August 5-6, 2026, via ABC News.

Hiroshima on Thursday marked the 81st anniversary of the U.S. atomic bombing of the western Japanese city, with its mayor criticizing major powers for waging wars and urging them to stop justifying the possession of nuclear weapons as a method of deterrence.

"Downplaying the inhumanity of nuclear weapons and accepting the use of force to pursue one's own prosperity risk cycles of violent retaliation that could ultimately result in another Hiroshima or Nagasaki," Mayor Kazumi Matsui said. Too many political leaders still consider nuclear deterrence a realistic approach and legitimize violence, which "simply pushes a world without nuclear weapons further into the distance," Matsui said in his peace declaration at the ceremony at the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park.

About 50,000 people, including representatives from some 120 countries and regions including the United States and Iran, attended the ceremony. A minute of silence was held while a peace bell rang out at 8:15 a.m., the time when a U.S. B-29 dropped the bomb on the city in 1945.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, attending the memorial for the first time as prime minister, said she will take "a realistic approach" toward achieving a world where nuclear weapons are never used, within the framework of the UN's treaty to prevent the spread of nuclear weapons. The ceremony comes amid speculation that Takaichi's hawkish government, which supports nuclear deterrence, may ease Japan's postwar three non-nuclear principles when it compiles new security and defense documents later this year.

The bombing of Hiroshima on Aug. 6, 1945, destroyed the city and killed 140,000 people; a second bomb dropped three days later on Nagasaki killed 70,000. The number of survivors has fallen to 91,105, about a quarter of the original number, with their average age exceeding 86.

UN Secretary-General António Guterres said in a statement: "The horror of nuclear weapons is just one lesson of Hiroshima. Another is the need to build a world in which the threat or use of these weapons becomes unthinkable."
