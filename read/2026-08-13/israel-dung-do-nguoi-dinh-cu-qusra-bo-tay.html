Israeli troops clash with settlers besieging Palestinian homes in occupied West Bank

Armed settlers blocking several roads, despite IDF declaration that area a closed military zone. Mark Weiss in Jerusalem, Wed Aug 12 2026 (The Irish Times, additional reporting Reuters).

Israeli soldiers clashed with Jewish settlers in the occupied West Bank on Wednesday in an attempt to break a three-day siege on the Palestinian village of Qusra, near Nablus.

The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said they had removed a structure from which extremist settlers had been restricting access to three Palestinian families, but footage showed some settlers remained at the location after the demolition. Armed settlers also blocked some roads, despite an IDF declaration that the area is a closed military zone.

Qusra council leader Abdel Azim Wadi said large numbers of settlers were continuing to arrive and described the situation as chaotic. A statement by the army characterised the settlers' actions as "illegal, reprehensible and unacceptable activity that harms the residents of the areas and disrupts their daily lives".

More than 300 military reservists signed a petition on Wednesday calling on IDF chief of staff Lieut Gen Eyal Zamir to take decisive action against what they describe as "Jewish terrorism" in the West Bank.

Aysha Hassan, a Palestinian woman who lives in one of the three besieged homes, said settlers set the outpost up four days ago and that the Palestinians have been effectively trapped there since. "They tried to break the gate," to the house, Hassan said, "and started throwing stones at us, cursing and insulting us. We [are] dying of fear, extremely terrified."

Ramiz Alakbarov, UN deputy special co-ordinator for the Middle East peace process, told the UN security council the West Bank was at "breaking point", saying settler violence has reached unprecedented levels, with more than 1,430 incidents in 2026. He said 3,800 Palestinian residents have been displaced due to the actions of settlers.

Israel's UN ambassador, Danny Danon, rejected the claim that Israeli settlements in the West Bank were an obstacle to peace, describing them as "strong, beautiful communities". "The security council needs to understand one simple truth: we are not going anywhere," he said.
