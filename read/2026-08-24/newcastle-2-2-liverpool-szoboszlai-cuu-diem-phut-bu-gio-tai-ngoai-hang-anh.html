Dominik Szoboszlai's 99th minute penalty gave Liverpool a second equalizer in a 2-2 draw with new-look Newcastle United at St. James' Park on Sunday in a collision of new managers.

The Magpies led early through Anthony Elanga but saw the score line level when Cody Gakpo scored a classy low goal early in the second half.

Joe Willock came off the bench and had a quick answer for Newcastle, and their 2-1 lead nearly made it to the end, but Victor Munoz won a penalty off Lewis Hall in the sixth minute of stoppage time after a minimum of five were added to the 90.

Liverpool struggle out wide, Newcastle's young midfield gets by

Both of Liverpool's fullbacks arrived in dangerous places a-plenty. Yet they did it at the expense of their defensive positioning, and Newcastle found good fortune in the spaces behind them. The biggest problem here is that Matthias Jaissle acknowledged after the game that they felt that's where their joy would be found and it was on both goals. It was worse on Jeremie Frimpong's song thanks to some struggles from young Rio Ngumoha, and Liverpool will have to sort out their wide areas if they want to challenge for silverware this season. Fortunately for the Reds, new signing Jeremy Jacquet looked plenty good with the ball and can save Virgil van Dijk some legwork this season.

Newcastle, on the other hand, deserved a point and it wouldn't have been too harsh on the visitors had the Magpies claimed all three on offer. And teenager Sean Steur combined with Lewis Miley to give Newcastle an adequate midfield in the side's first game since selling Bruno Guimaraes and Sandro Tonali (They were also without injured Joelinton to complete the invisible trio). Both players tired and both Jacob Ramsey and Aladji Bamba weren't bad in their stead, but Newcastle simply must buy a No. 1, no-doubt, already-proven center midfielder before the end of the week. If Man City is really willing to move Nico Gonzalez on to another side, that could do the trick nicely for Matthias Jaissle. The new boss' ideas did impress against Liverpool.

Reds shouldn't sell Cody Gakpo

The goal was terrific, but Cody Gakpo's day was essentially Liverpool's attacking day. His size and instincts work well with Florian Wirtz, who was also decent, and it's just so wild that the Reds are considering selling the Dutchman — especially inside of the division. Yes, they may land Bradley Barcola or another first-choice option to move Alexander Isak out wide, but they'll need a player like Gakpo more than a young buck like Rio Ngumoha. In fact, if he's for sale, Newcastle should muscle into the race.
