Infantino holds crisis meeting in Morocco as FIFA stake sale fallout continues

By Reuters (Rohith Nair). Published August 5, 2026, CNBC Africa.

Gianni Infantino held a crisis meeting with top FIFA officials in Morocco on Wednesday as he looked to shore up his position in the wake of the collapse of his gambit to sell off commercial rights to the World Cup. The FIFA president has been the subject of a torrent of criticism since he floated the idea of the new commercial rights body to FIFA's 211 member associations last week.

The proposed sale of 20% of the body to private investment to raise $4.2 billion, and the involvement of a company with family links to U.S. President Donald Trump, were among the most-criticised flaws in the plan. His backdown last Friday has done little to assuage critics, most notably European confederation UEFA, who accused him of selling the soul of the game and said they no longer have confidence in his leadership — casting a shadow over Infantino's expected re-election for a fourth term at the FIFA Congress in Morocco in March.

FIFA Secretary General Mattias Grafstrom sent an internal memo lamenting the "sad and reproachable series of events" that led to the project being "permanently abandoned," while FIFA Chief Operating Officer Kevin Lamour said staff had been "deceived" over the plan, which he described as "the project of one person." Infantino's senior adviser Carlos Cordeiro resigned in protest, and former Arsenal manager Arsene Wenger, FIFA's chief of Global Football Development, distanced himself from the plan on Tuesday.

According to a report in the Times of London, UEFA, CONCACAF and the Asian Football Confederation are determined to force Infantino out of office by paralysing FIFA and even starting up their own competitions if he refuses to go. Leading African football administrators have sent messages of support, and Infantino currently faces no declared challenger for the March election, though Jordan FA President Prince Ali bin Hussein — a two-time former candidate — accused FIFA of "blackmail" over the crisis.
