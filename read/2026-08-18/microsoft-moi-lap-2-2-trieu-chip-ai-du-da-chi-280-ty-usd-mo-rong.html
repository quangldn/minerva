A Guardian investigation reports an apparent gap between what Microsoft has said about its AI capacity and the number of advanced AI chips it actually has in operation. By Aisha Down and Ed Zitron, Mon 17 Aug 2026.

According to internal documents seen by the Guardian, Microsoft has roughly 2.2 million AI chips installed across its global datacentres — a figure the paper describes as less than half what several experts expected, given that the company is in the middle of a $280bn (GBP 208bn) expansion and had reportedly targeted 1.8m installed chips by the end of 2024. Sources inside the company told the Guardian the total chip count has "barely moved" over the past year.

The gap matters because the AI race is measured largely in datacentre power. Microsoft says it has added about 5GW of capacity over the past two years and now runs hundreds of datacentres on five continents; an internal 2024 presentation reportedly already claimed 5GW installed, implying a current total near 10GW. On the Guardian's methodology — reviewed with academics Shaolei Ren (UC Riverside) and Abdeltawab Hendawi (University of Rhode Island) — 10GW of AI datacentre capacity would translate to roughly 6.4 million GPUs once cooling overhead and non-AI components in each server rack are subtracted.

Ren argued Microsoft's separately published, third-party audited sustainability reports point to a much smaller AI footprint, closer to 1.2GW in 2024 — but even that lower baseline plus 5GW of new build would require around 4 million chips. He also cautioned that announcing power capacity on paper is far easier than energising it and putting it to work computing in the same quarter.

Several flagship projects appear to be only partly live. Microsoft's Fairwater complex in Wisconsin was described by CEO Satya Nadella in April as "going live," yet satellite imagery analysed by Epoch AI suggests only part of the site is operational, and in May the company acknowledged to a Wisconsin newspaper that it was not yet online. The internal document also shows fewer Nvidia Blackwell chips than expected: Jensen Huang said last March that Nvidia's top four customers had ordered 3.6m Blackwells, which would put Microsoft near 1m — it has under half that installed.

Nadella himself has pointed to the bottleneck, saying on a podcast late last year that the constraint is not chip supply but power and "warm shells" — finished, energised buildings — to plug hardware into, leaving inventory potentially idle.

Microsoft told the Guardian it does not report volumes of specific chips, that its datacentres mix custom silicon with AMD, Intel and Nvidia parts across multiple generations, and that the estimates shared with it were inaccurate and drew wrong conclusions from incorrect assumptions. It did not specify which figures were wrong. Nvidia did not respond to a request for comment. The broader difficulty, the report notes, is that Nvidia discloses almost nothing about who buys its chips and in what quantity, and its customers disclose little more — making the true state of the AI build-out hard for anyone outside those companies to verify.
