01:45 GMT — Agency ‘not planning on going anywhere’ despite Israeli seizure, says UNRWA chief

We have more from Christian Saunders, the head of the UN agency for Palestinian refugees, UNRWA.

In an interview with Al Jazeera, Saunders pushed back against Israeli claims regarding the seized Qalandiya training centre after authorities alleged the property was owned by the Jewish National Fund and allocated to the Jerusalem Municipality for a planned complex, citing October 2024 legislation restricting UNRWA’s activities.

UNRWA firmly rejected the claims, maintaining that the centre is a UN facility protected under international law.

“We’re a humanitarian organisation; we’re a development organisation. We are given our mandate by the United Nations General Assembly to provide critical services,” Saunders said. He added that UNRWA would continue doing so “until the General Assembly decides otherwise, or there is a durable solution to the Palestine question”.

Saunders said UNRWA was doing what it could to protect its staff and facilities through security assessments, training and risk mitigation.

He said the Israelis’ use of force was ill-advised and that the only solution was through dialogue.

“I think it is long overdue for the Israelis and the Palestinians to sit down and to resolve these issues. We’re not planning on going anywhere,” Saunders said.

01:00 GMT — Israel aims to make Palestinian refugee issue ‘go away’, UNRWA chief tells Al Jazeera

Christian Saunders, the head of the UN agency for Palestinian refugees, UNRWA, says the Israeli government’s raid on the organisation’s Qalandiya vocational training centre was an attempt to “eliminate the presence of the United Nations” in the occupied Palestinian territory.

Saunders told Al Jazeera that while the raid displaced UNRWA employees and left at least seven Palestinians injured by live ammunition, the broader goal is to make the refugee issue disappear.

“If UNRWA is defunded and eliminated, then they are hoping that the issue of the refugees, the Palestine refugees, will go away,” Saunders said. “Which means that the right of return, reparations, and compensation would go away as well.”

Israeli forces seized the facility in the Kafr Aqab neighbourhood near occupied East Jerusalem on Tuesday, with far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir participating and declaring that there was “no place for the agency in Jerusalem and Israel”.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu defended the evacuation as lawful under Israeli legislation and accused the agency of aiding Hamas in the October 7, 2023 attacks on Israel.

Saunders expressed “disappointment” over the impact on local young people, noting that the facility previously provided essential education and training.

Some “340 young people are not going to get the education, not going to get the training they need”, Saunders said, stressing that the programme gives students “the skills and an opportunity to lead a full and dignified life”.

00:20 GMT — Tehran diplomacy gathers pace as Qatari PM visits to de-escalate tensions

By Sinem Koseoglu, reporting from Tehran, Iran.

There’s a growing sense in Tehran tonight that diplomacy is gathering pace despite a broader agreement remaining some way off.

Today, Qatar’s foreign minister and prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani, is going to pay a visit to Tehran to meet with high-level Iranian officials.

The aim of the visit, as per the Qataris, is to de-escalate tensions and create a platform for dialogue.

This visit comes after a very busy week featuring visits from Pakistan’s army chief, followed by Oman’s foreign minister. Qatar hopes that all these discussions may create a platform for dialogue between Iran and the United States.

We heard Omani and Iranian officials saying that they agreed on a framework for a temporary shipping route in the Strait of Hormuz, which is the most urgent problem in the region for now, alongside efforts to clear mines from the waterway.

Qatar expects these negotiations may open the way to resume talks between Tehran and Washington. However, these expectations should be measured as both sides have different points of view when it comes to communication.

Even so, this is an important development. After months of military confrontation, a diplomatic channel exists between these countries, though Iran points out that these talks shouldn’t be taken as a direct resumption of bilateral negotiations, as they are just talks with mediators.

These are the main differences between the US and Iran. There isn’t a big expectation for an imminent breakthrough in this conflict right now, but all parties hope that diplomacy builds a new platform for dialogue and, hopefully, for the resumption of formal negotiations.
