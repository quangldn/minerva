A close ally of Iran's supreme leader said President Donald Trump's current rhetoric about the war stems from desperation, vowing Iran "will not submit." The US is preparing to ramp up economic sanctions on Tehran, with further details set to be announced Monday.

Iranian state media reports the country will allow Iraqi oil tankers to travel through the critical Strait of Hormuz waterway. The number of ships using the strait stands at 20% of the pre-war average, according to a CNN analysis of figures from the UK's maritime agency.

Tehran's security chief vowed to "neutralize the economic war" with the United States and threatened to halt oil flow out of the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's state-affiliated Press TV reported.

"America is backed into a corner. Either they honor their commitments or they will have to struggle to get out of that corner, which will make the situation worse," Mohsen Rezaei said late Saturday in a televised speech reported by Press TV.

Iran is allowing a number of Iraqi oil tankers to pass through the Strait of Hormuz, in the latest example of Tehran granting selective access to the vital waterway. However, Rezaei, the secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, threatened to shut down all traffic if any of Iran's neighboring countries enter President Donald Trump's economic war.

"If they start such an action, we will not allow a single drop of oil to pass through the Persian Gulf. Oil will leave neither through the Strait of Hormuz nor through the Persian Gulf."

Rezaei said Iran has shipped 70 million barrels of oil "in the past one or two months alone," suggesting that was despite the US blockade of Iranian ports that resumed in mid July.

Meanwhile, the US Department of Energy said oil traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has averaged between 8 million and 9 million barrels per day as ships attempt "dark transits" through the waterway.

Hossein Taeb, a close ally of Iran's supreme leader, said US President Donald Trump's current rhetoric stems from "desperation" as he vowed Tehran "will not submit" to the US, Iranian semi-official media reported.

A former intelligence chief who Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei appointed as head of Iran's volunteer paramilitary militia Basij earlier this month, Taeb said Trump's rhetoric is "not based on initiative; rather, it stems from desperation," according to the country's Fars News Agency.

"The enemy has lost its initiative and is trying to change the situation through other methods," Fars cited the hardline militia figure as saying, after Trump earlier threatened to unleash an "economic D-Day" on Iran, sparking backlash from Tehran.

Taeb also said Iran would not reopen the critical Strait of Hormuz unless "our conditions are fulfilled."

US President Donald Trump played down the war with Iran, calling it a "little detour," with his administration set to ramp up economic sanctions on Tehran. "Look, it's a Friday night. We have plenty of time, right? What the hell do I have to do? Go back, bomb Iran a little bit more?" Trump said at a rally in South Carolina.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will hold a news conference Monday after pledging to apply measures that have "never been seen" on Iran. Iran pushed back on Trump's threat of an "economic D-Day," calling it "a recipe for an abysmal return to full-scale classic colonialism."
