Sei Young Kim Leads FM Championship After Flawless 64

Brendon Elliott, Athlon Sports — August 27, 2026

Emily Kristine Pedersen and Miyu Yamashita sit one shot back after reaching the same score through two very different rounds at TPC Boston.

Sei Young Kim was even after her first hole Thursday. By the time she walked off the seventh green, she was 6 under and the FM Championship's opening-day record was in play. Kim birdied Nos. 2 and 3, added two more at Nos. 5 and 6, then chipped in for eagle at the par-5 seventh. An outward 30 eventually carried her to an 8-under 64.

It was the lowest opening round in tournament history and gave Kim a one-shot lead over Emily Kristine Pedersen and Miyu Yamashita at TPC Boston in Norton, Massachusetts. Both chasers shot 65. Their scorecards had little else in common.

Pedersen hit 16 greens while managing a wrist that still limits how many balls she can hit. Yamashita made 10 birdies, more than anyone in the field, yet needed all but three of them to offset a double bogey and a closing bogey. Kim combined one torrid stretch with a bogey-free card and 24 putts.

Kim Turned One Miss Into an Eagle

Kim's six-hole burst included four birdies and the eagle at No. 7. She drove just right of the fairway, played a hybrid from about 190 meters and finished roughly 3 yards right of the hole before chipping in.

The leader made pars on the next six holes. Birdies at Nos. 14 and 18 completed a 64 that looked different from the round's opening rush, but no less useful. Kim hit 12 of 14 fairways and 13 of 18 greens, then prevented any of those five missed greens from becoming a bogey.

"I feel like this course is a little softer, greens are a little softer than last year," Kim said. The rain-softened conditions gave players more freedom to aim at flags, she explained, though the doglegs and rough still placed a premium on putting the tee shot in play.

The 64 also continued a strong stretch. Kim shot 64 in the third round of last week's CPKC Women's Open, where she finished third. The 13-time LPGA Tour winner has six top-10 finishes in 15 starts this season and is ninth in the Race to the CME Globe.

Pedersen Made Limited Practice Count

Pedersen's 65 was her lowest LPGA Tour round since the third round of the 2024 Honda LPGA Thailand. It also put her in her best post-round position since she was tied for second after the third round of the 2023 Dana Open.

Pedersen spent the first four months of this season recovering from wrist surgery. Her arm was immobilized in a cast for eight weeks. She returned at the Mizuho Americas Open, then missed six cuts in eight starts before Thursday.

"I still can't hit to the extent that I normally do," Pedersen said. "I've always been someone that practices a lot and I'm still a little bit on a ball count."

She also changed her putter and grip before the round. The immediate result was 28 putts, enough to turn 16 greens into a 65. Her only bogey came at No. 13; she followed it with birdies on three of the final five holes, including Nos. 17 and 18.

Yamashita Answered a Double Bogey With a 30

Yamashita arrived from Canada after winning last week's CPKC Women's Open and tying the LPGA Tour's 72-hole scoring record. She made three birdies before the par-3 eighth, where a double bogey dropped her back to 1 under. She parred the ninth to turn in 35, then started the back nine with four consecutive birdies. After a par at No. 14, she added three more birdies from Nos. 15 through 17. A bogey at the par-5 18th left her with a back-nine 30 and a share of second.

Yamashita hit all 14 fairways and needed 24 putts.

Somi Lee sits alone in fourth after a 66. Eight players share fifth at 4 under, while Nelly Korda, Lydia Ko, Jeeno Thitikul and Rose Zhang each opened with 72 and are tied for 39th.
