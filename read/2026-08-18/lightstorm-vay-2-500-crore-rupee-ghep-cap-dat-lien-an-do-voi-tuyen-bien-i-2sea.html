Lightstorm secured a Rs 2,500 crore, fully underwritten 10-year debt facility from IndusInd Bank to accelerate an AI-focused terrestrial and subsea fiber corridor linking India with Singapore and Malaysia.

Lightstorm has secured a Rs 2,500 crore (Rs 25 billion) fully underwritten debt facility from IndusInd Bank to accelerate a new high-capacity fiber corridor linking India's emerging AI infrastructure clusters with Singapore and Malaysia. The 10-year financing will support what Lightstorm calls its "AI Superhighway," built around a utility-grade, deep-trenched terrestrial network across southern India and an international extension through the planned I-2SEA submarine cable system. Lightstorm says the architecture is being engineered for low latency and deterministic performance between data centers, cloud hubs and distributed GPU infrastructure. The company also states that it carries a CARE AA- rating with a stable outlook.

The domestic network is intended to connect major AI and data center zones across India and feed directly into I-2SEA, creating an integrated terrestrial-subsea route toward Singapore and Kuala Lumpur. I-2SEA is being developed under a Joint Build Agreement involving Lightstorm, Microsoft, Singtel and Tata Communications, with Lightstorm serving as majority owner. The approximately 3,600-km system will land in India at Machilipatnam, providing a short subsea path toward Hyderabad, and at a new South Chennai location. NEC is the system supplier and ASEAN Cableship is the marine installation partner. Lightstorm is targeting Ready-for-Service in Q4 2029 and says the combined subsea and terrestrial route is being designed to provide a high-performance Singapore/Malaysia-to-Hyderabad path for AI workloads.

The financing expands an infrastructure platform that already spans more than 30,000 km of terrestrial fiber, 21,000 km of subsea fiber and more than 100 connected data centers globally, according to Lightstorm. Its current network includes 18+ cloud on-ramps and more than 3,000 Tbps of deployed capacity, with optical services at 10G, 100G and 400G and a roadmap extending toward 1.6T architectures. Lightstorm combines this physical infrastructure through its SmartNet AI Fabric with Polarin, a software-defined Network-as-a-Service control layer for provisioning, monitoring and scaling connectivity. The company is backed by infrastructure investment manager I Squared Capital.

Scott McGibbony, Group CFO of Lightstorm, said: "India is building the compute infrastructure of the AI era, and the network connecting that compute needs to be built to the same standard."

Analysis

The significant technical development is the attempt to treat terrestrial DCI and international subsea capacity as a single AI transport architecture rather than as separate network domains. Large distributed GPU environments can generate traffic patterns that are highly sensitive not only to average latency but also to jitter, packet loss, path variability and congestion. Lightstorm is positioning SmartNet around those requirements while using Polarin as the programmable service-control layer across the underlying infrastructure.

I-2SEA also illustrates how AI infrastructure investment is influencing the geography of submarine networks. Its Machilipatnam landing is specifically designed to shorten the path toward Hyderabad, while South Chennai adds a second Indian landing. This links two major Indian data center regions directly toward Singapore and Kuala Lumpur rather than treating cable landing locations simply as international gateways. Lightstorm says the buried sections of I-2SEA will target three-meter burial depth, adding physical protection to the route design.

The project fits into a broader expansion of India-centered international connectivity. Google is developing new India-centered subsea routes as part of its America-India Connect infrastructure program, while existing India-Singapore systems are receiving higher-capacity coherent upgrades. Converge Digest has also tracked expanding AI data center capacity in Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Visakhapatnam. The common infrastructure trend is that large-scale compute deployments are increasingly being planned alongside terrestrial DCI, cable landing diversity and international fiber capacity rather than as isolated data center projects.
