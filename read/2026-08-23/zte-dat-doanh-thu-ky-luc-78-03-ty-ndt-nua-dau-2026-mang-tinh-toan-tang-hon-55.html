ZTE Shifts Toward AI Infrastructure, Revenue Rises 9%

ZTE reported record H1 2026 revenue of RMB 78.03 billion (~US$11.61 billion), with computing revenue rising more than 55% and data center solutions growing over 90%.

ZTE reported record first-half 2026 revenue of RMB 78.03 billion (approximately US$11.61 billion), up 9.0% year over year, as rapid growth in AI computing, enterprise infrastructure and international markets offset weaker spending by Chinese telecom operators. Computing revenue increased more than 55% and expanded to 35.1% of company revenue, compared with 27% in the first quarter. Server and storage revenue increased nearly 60%, while data center solutions revenue rose more than 90%. Net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 2.75 billion (approximately US$409 million), while adjusted net profit excluding extraordinary items was RMB 2.22 billion (approximately US$330 million).

The shift in ZTE’s revenue mix is increasingly visible across its operating segments. Government & Enterprise Business revenue jumped 43.5% to RMB 27.63 billion (approximately US$4.11 billion), representing 35.4% of total revenue, supported by demand for servers, storage and intelligent computing infrastructure. Operator Network remained the largest individual segment at RMB 31.77 billion (approximately US$4.73 billion), or 40.7% of revenue, despite lower domestic telecom infrastructure investment. Consumer Business generated RMB 18.63 billion (approximately US$2.77 billion), up 8.1%. International revenue increased 33.1% to RMB 27.87 billion (approximately US$4.15 billion), driven by 5G, fiber deployments and AI computing infrastructure.

ZTE is positioning its “Connectivity + Computing” strategy around full-stack AI infrastructure spanning chips, servers, networking, software and data center energy systems. The company highlighted its OEX-architecture SuperPod, which uses a backplane-free design for multi-chip collaboration and open decoupling, along with an AIDC architecture integrating computing infrastructure with power and energy management. ZTE said its servers and storage, data center switches and data center solutions are achieving scaled deployments across internet, financial, power and public-sector customers in China while expanding internationally. R&D spending totaled RMB 10.93 billion (approximately US$1.63 billion), and ZTE reported approximately 98,000 patent applications worldwide, including nearly 6,000 related to AI.

Analysis

The most significant change in ZTE’s business is the speed at which computing is approaching the scale of its traditional telecom infrastructure operation. Computing represented 27% of revenue when ZTE reported Q1 2026 results; across the full first half, it reached 35.1%. ZTE has also introduced a 576×800G chassis switch for AI clusters and a Scale-Across architecture targeting distributed computing over distances up to 300 kilometers. The H1 results indicate that these infrastructure initiatives are developing alongside rapidly increasing server, storage and data center systems revenue.

The revenue mix also illustrates the economics of that transition. ZTE generated record revenue even as domestic telecommunications infrastructure investment declined, but profitability remains under pressure as the business shifts toward computing and other product categories. Net profit of RMB 2.75 billion is equivalent to roughly 3.5% of revenue, while R&D spending of RMB 10.93 billion represents approximately 14% of revenue. ZTE specifically attributed gross-margin pressure to industry-cycle transitions and the changing revenue mix.

The international expansion is another important component of the strategy. International revenue increased 33.1% and now represents 35.7% of company sales, supported by 5G, fiber broadband and AI computing infrastructure. ZTE therefore increasingly spans three infrastructure domains: traditional carrier networking, AI/data center computing and end-user devices. Its stated technical strategy connects those domains through common AI, silicon, networking and software capabilities rather than treating AI computing as a standalone server business.
