Meta and Nvidia stake claim in open-weight AI race amid Chinese leadership

Meta and Nvidia this week published artificial intelligence models with downloadable weights, a visible step in a broader U.S. industry effort to preserve and expand an open-weight AI ecosystem needed to compete with powerful models coming from Chinese labs.

Meta announced the release of Muse Glimmer and said it would open the weights for its Muse Spark 1.2 model, while Nvidia published Nemotron 3.5 Lightning and described its Nemotron family as "truly open source," releasing model weights, training datasets and techniques for inspection.

Chinese developers have produced some of the most powerful open-weight models in recent months, including labs such as Moonshot AI and DeepSeek, along with Alibaba's Qwen — reshaping the competitive landscape and prompting security concerns in Washington.

A coalition of roughly two dozen U.S. companies, including Nvidia, Microsoft and Meta, published an open letter in late July urging policymakers not to impose "premature restrictions" on open-weight AI.
