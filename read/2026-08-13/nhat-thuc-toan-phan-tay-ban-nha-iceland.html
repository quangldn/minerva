WATCH LIVE: Total solar eclipse darkens skies over Spain, Iceland

Across a swath of northern and central Spain, millions of people had some of mainland Europe's best views of the celestial spectacle. By Suman Naishadham and Teresa Medrano, Associated Press. Science, Aug 12, 2026.

MEDINACELI, Spain (AP) — Spaniards rushed to buy specialized glasses to view a rare total solar eclipse Wednesday, as authorities warned people flocking to rural areas to avoid anything that could spark new wildfires.

Across a swath of northern and central Spain, millions of people had some of mainland Europe's best views of the celestial spectacle. It began around sunset, when the sun, moon and Earth aligned, briefly bringing parts of the Iberian Peninsula, plus slivers of Greenland and Iceland, into darkness. Totality — when the moon completely covers the sun's light — lasted less than 2 1/2 minutes, reaching its maximum duration off Iceland's west coast; by the time it reached Spain, totality had dwindled to about a minute. Spain saw its first major total solar eclipse since 1905.

The eclipse drew at least an additional half a million visitors, authorities said. Demand for hotel rooms along the path of totality, including in the northern cities of A Coruña, Bilbao and Santiago de Compostela, soared. Spain's Interior Ministry said 350 official viewing sites were set up across the sparsely populated interior, often referred to as "Empty Spain," while access to another 123 sites was limited or restricted because of environmental conditions or wildfire evacuation risk.

Only weeks earlier, the most destructive wildfire in Spanish history devoured 500 square kilometres and forced the evacuation of tens of thousands of people in Ávila, in central Spain. "High temperatures, the accumulated dryness of vegetation, and the intense pressure on natural areas due to projected travel necessitate extreme preventive measures," Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska said. More than 33,500 law enforcement officers were deployed to observation areas and access points.

In Reykjavík, rooms were averaging more than $1,000 for the night as Icelanders prepared for their first total solar eclipse since 1954 and the first visible from the capital since 1433, though cloudy skies were forecast across most of the island when the eclipse peaked. Iceland expected up to 20,000 additional overseas visitors during an already busy peak tourist period.
