Fringe First Awards 2026: Here are all of this year's Fringe First Award winners so far

Recognising the best of new writing at the Edinburgh Fringe, here is every winner of the Fringe First Awards 2026.

The Scotsman’s Fringe Firsts are the longest running awards of the Edinburgh Fringe, with this winners being rewarded for outstanding writing.

Sponsored this year by Queen Margaret University, our expert panel of judges recognise the best of new writing premiered at the Edinburgh Fringe, with the winners announced each Friday of the festival.

Established in 1973, past Fringe First winners include Rowan Atkinson, Stephen Fry, and Billy Connolly, as well as the likes of Phoebe Waller-Bridge for the original stage version of Fleabag, and Richard Gadd for Baby Reindeer.

Following the first two ceremonies - and with the final coming up - there are now a handful of award winners this year, including an adaptation of Andrew O’Hagan’s acclaimed book Mayflies and a reworking of Shakespeare’s Hamlet.

Here are all of the 2026 Fringe First award winners, so far.

1. Amnesia

A show about a dementia carer whose best friend is choosing to move to a new city, leaving her behind, Amnesia took home an award during week two. In her review of the show, critic Susan Mansfield wrote: “Kimberley Hart-Simpson, best known as Nicky Wheatley in Coronation Street, writes and performs this one-woman show about a dementia carer in Rhyl, the town where she grew up. Hart-Simpson has written a complex, fully rounded character, in a play about the people who don’t get out, who do vital work like caring for those with dementia, and are left with the lingering notion that their lives are somehow not enough.” — Amnesia is at Bunker Two, Pleasance Courtyard (Venue 33) until August 31.

2. Bigfoot Ripped My Dog In Half I Saw It

Written and starring Xhloe and Natasha, Bigfoot Ripped My Dog In Half I Saw It was one of the earliest 2026 Fringe First award winners. The play follows two teens who perform fake Bigfoot sightings in their Appalachian town — up until a neighbour's dog turns up torn to pieces. Selected by our highly experienced panel of critics, chief theatre critic Joyce McMillan wrote in her review: “these two remarkable writer-performers take just an hour to create a community that seems like a near-perfect metaphor for the current state of America, and for much of western society. Bereft for months of drinkable running water after a traumatic flood, the town is already stressed and on edge when two teenagers start to spin their tale of a gigantic creature in the headlights, frightening them off the road. The central thrust of this brilliantly conceived story soon becomes all too clear, in what must be one of the sharpest, most significant, and most breathtakingly executed physical theatre shows of the year.” Bigfoot Ripped My Dog In Half I Saw It is on at Summerhall (Venue 26) until August 30.

3. HAM

In her review of HAM — inspired by Shakespeare’s Hamlet — Sally Stott wrote about this Fringe First winning play: “To be or not to be vegan – that is the question,” especially when, as well as reducing the number of animals killed, it could also help to save the planet. In this reworking of Shakespeare’s Hamlet, Mary Higgins and Ell Potter debate the issue with an arresting combination of humour and horror. It’s a concept that might fill any meat eaters with fear, but in true Shakespearian style, both sides of the argument are debated with equal flair, at least until the end, with the two likeable performers on their way to being massacred.” HAM is on at the Old Lab, Summerhall (Venue 26) until August 31.

4. Mayflies

Mayflies made its world premiere at the 2026 Edinburgh Fringe, with Grid Iron’s adaptation of Andrew O'Hagan's novel winning a Fringe First prize. Joyce McMillan writes: “Edinburgh’s brilliant site-specific theatre specialists Grid Iron tackle the subject of mortality in characteristic style, opening up a fabulous old warehouse space on The Shore for their new staging of Andrew O’Hagan’s acclaimed 2020 book Mayflies, a love story about the profound friendship between two men, Tully and Jimmy, who grew up together in a working-class Ayrshire town, and are bonded forever by their intense shared teenage experiences in music and culture. When, 30 years on, Tully calls Jimmy to say that he has had a terminal diagnosis, and that he wants help in arranging to end his own life, Jimmy feels compelled to step up. Mayflies offers an almost flawless evocation of O’Hagan’s beautifully told story about the gentler rhythms of male friendship, in an age when traditional masculinity is often viewed as toxic.” Mayflies is at Brown's of Leith (Venue 320) until August 30.
