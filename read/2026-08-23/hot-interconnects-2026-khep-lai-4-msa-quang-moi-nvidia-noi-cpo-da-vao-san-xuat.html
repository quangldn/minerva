AI Networking Converges Around Scale Up, Scale Out and Scale Across

Hot Interconnects 2026 highlighted the convergence of Scale Up, Scale Out and Scale Across networking for AI infrastructure, with keynotes from Meta, NVIDIA and Ciena, four optical MSA talks, record attendance and nine accepted technical papers.

Hot Interconnects 2026 online symposium closed its main technical program with a clear message about where high-performance interconnect architecture is heading: AI infrastructure is forcing the industry to rethink networking simultaneously across Scale Up, Scale Out and Scale Across. The two-day IEEE main program (not including tutorial sessions on Friday) drew more than 1,500 registrations and over 1,000 live attendees, with three keynotes, a six-expert panel, four optical MSA lightning talks, nine accepted technical papers, eight sponsor presentations and a six-session tutorial program.

The conference theme — the roles of Scale Up, Scale Out and Scale Across — ran through presentations from hyperscalers, networking vendors, optical companies and standards organizations. In his closing remarks, Hot Interconnects Organizing Committee Vice Chair David Ozog noted that although the underlying technologies can differ substantially across these domains, their boundaries are becoming less distinct and many of the engineering concepts are shared.

The optimization problem increasingly spans latency, throughput, energy per bit, copper versus optical connectivity, power efficiency, message rates and system cost. That convergence was particularly visible in the three conference keynotes from Meta, NVIDIA and Ciena, as well as four lightning talks covering new optical Multi-Source Agreements announced at OFC.

Three Keynotes Define the AI Interconnect Challenge

Meta — Senior Director of Networking Omar Baldonado described an AI infrastructure architecture that has progressed from approximately 24,000–32,000 GPU clusters to a 129,000-GPU system and now toward multi-building, gigawatt-scale deployments. Meta divides the problem into Scale Up, Scale Out and Scale Across, but Baldonado emphasized that networking cannot be designed independently from GPUs, CPUs, PCIe, CXL, memory, storage, cooling, power or workload scheduling.

NVIDIA — Senior Vice President of Networking Gilad Shainer argued that an AI factory has to operate as a single computing system supported by multiple purpose-built network infrastructures. NVIDIA's architecture uses NVLink for Scale Up, Spectrum-X Ethernet or InfiniBand for Scale Out, Spectrum-XGS for Scale Across, and BlueField-4 for infrastructure processing and an emerging context-memory storage tier. Shainer also said Spectrum-X Ethernet Photonics co-packaged optics technology is now in production.

Ciena — Senior Director of Product Line Management Bilal Riaz described an AI networking roadmap spanning 6.4T CPX optical engines, 204.8T switching, 448G SerDes, active copper, liquid-cooled optics and increasingly optical Scale Up connectivity. The keynote illustrated why the distinction between conventional data center networking and optical transport is becoming less useful for the largest AI systems.

Optical MSAs Target the Next Interconnect Boundaries

Four lightning talks highlighted industry efforts to standardize optical connectivity for AI infrastructure. The OCI MSA is targeting optical Scale Up connectivity as copper reach becomes increasingly difficult to maintain across large accelerator domains. XPO is developing an Extreme Short Reach pluggable architecture with a roadmap toward 25.6T modules. Open CPX is defining socketed optical engines intended to bring serviceable, modular optics close to switching and compute silicon. The SDM4 MCF MSA is developing four-core multicore fiber for short-reach AI Scale Out applications, targeting as much as four times the fiber density of conventional single-core fiber.

Converge Digest: 10 Reports from Hot Interconnects 2026

1. Meta — Scale Up, Scale Out and Scale Across. Omar Baldonado detailed Meta's progression toward gigawatt-scale, multi-building AI infrastructure and explained why network architecture, workload scheduling, power, optics and physical data center design increasingly have to be co-designed.

2. Lightmatter — BiDi DWDM for AI Interconnect. Nick Harris examined bidirectional DWDM as a path to increased connectivity density for large AI systems.

3. Cisco — Ethernet for Multi-Tenant AI Fabrics. Cisco outlined an Ethernet architecture for shared AI infrastructure incorporating job-aware multi-tenancy, multipath transport, adaptive routing and SRv6-based deterministic paths.

4. Broadcom — Ethernet Across AI Fabrics. Broadcom mapped Ethernet across Scale Up, Scale Out and Scale Across, including Tomahawk switching, Thor NICs and Multi-path Reliable Connections.

5. NVIDIA — Gigascale AI Network Architecture. Gilad Shainer described an AI factory architecture spanning NVLink, Spectrum-X, Spectrum-XGS, BlueField-4, context-memory infrastructure and production CPO.

6. Ciena — CPO, 448G and the Post-DAC Network. Bilal Riaz outlined a roadmap toward 448G electrical interfaces, 6.4T optical engines, 204.8T switching, active copper, liquid cooling and greater use of optics inside AI Scale Up systems.

7. OCI MSA — Optical Scale Up. Meta's Drew Alduino presented an effort to create an interoperable optical connectivity architecture for Scale Up networks as accelerator domains grow beyond practical copper reach.

8. XPO MSA — Toward 25.6T Pluggables. XPO outlined an Extreme Short Reach optical architecture intended to preserve the operational advantages of pluggable optics while pushing toward a roadmap of 25.6T modules.

9. Open CPX MSA — Socketed Optical Engines. Open CPX is developing a common socketed optical-engine architecture aimed at placing optics closer to AI switching and compute silicon while retaining modularity and serviceability.

10. SDM4 MCF MSA — 4X Fiber Density. The SDM4 MCF MSA is developing a four-core multicore fiber specification for short-reach O-band AI data center Scale Out links, targeting as much as four times conventional fiber density.

Analysis

Hot Interconnects 2026 captured an important architectural transition in AI infrastructure: networking is moving closer to the compute architecture itself. Scale Up increasingly resembles a memory and compute fabric; Scale Out must deliver predictable performance across enormous accelerator populations; and Scale Across is extending distributed AI beyond the boundaries of individual buildings and data centers. The technologies differ, but the engineering problem is increasingly continuous.

The optical boundary is moving with it. Copper retains substantial advantages in power, cost and reliability at sufficiently short distances, but higher SerDes rates, larger accelerator domains and greater bandwidth density are progressively reducing the distances over which electrical interconnect remains practical. CPO, socketed optical engines, high-density pluggables, multicore fiber and optical Scale Up fabrics are therefore emerging as complementary approaches rather than a single replacement architecture.

Perhaps the most significant theme from HOTI 2026 was the amount of work now occurring at the interfaces between previously separate disciplines. GPU architecture, Ethernet, optical networking, chiplets, memory, packaging, cooling, power and data center construction increasingly interact in determining useful AI system performance. That makes the interconnect less of a component choice and more of a system-level architectural decision.
