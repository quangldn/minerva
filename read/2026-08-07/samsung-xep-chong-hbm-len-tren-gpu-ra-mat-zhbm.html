Samsung Stacks HBM Atop GPU, Breaking AI Chip Limits

Source: Seoul Economic Daily — Published August 5, 2026

Samsung Electronics has unveiled a next-generation memory architecture that vertically stacks high-bandwidth memory (HBM), an ultra-high-performance and high-efficiency DRAM chip, on top of an artificial intelligence (AI) accelerator. The strategy aims to solve the data bottleneck problem that plagues the AI era and to secure leadership in the rapidly growing application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) market.

Samsung Electronics showcased physical models of the three-dimensional (3D) memory architecture "zHBM" and "z-NAND-O" at "FMS 2026," which opened at the Santa Clara Convention Center in the United States on the 4th.

The name zHBM refers to the use of the third dimension—height (the Z-axis)—moving beyond the two-dimensional structure of width and length. It is a memory chip that stacks HBM on top of a graphics processing unit (GPU). In existing AI accelerators, HBM is placed next to the GPU that computes data, causing data bottlenecks such as computational delays due to the physical distance between the semiconductors. Samsung Electronics forecast that once zHBM is mass-produced and supplied, its performance will improve by up to eight times compared with the eighth-generation HBM5.

Kim Kyung-ryun, senior vice president of Samsung Electronics' DRAM Development Division, expressed his intent to lead the market through zHBM, which broke the "Memory Wall" of existing AI chips, and voiced confidence to the global semiconductor industry, saying, "Samsung is back."

Samsung Electronics also introduced, for the first time in the industry, "z-NAND-O," a next-generation NAND solution optimized for on-device AI environments, and "V10 BV-NAND," a 10th-generation V-NAND that vertically stacks more than 400 layers.
