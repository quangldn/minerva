Activist and educationist Sonam Wangchuk on Friday ended his 26-day hunger strike at Medanta Hospital in Gurugram after Prime Minister Narendra Modi issued a statement addressing key concerns raised by the protest movement. The decision came following a meeting with Union Health Minister J.P. Nadda and Minister of State in the Prime Minister's Office Dr. Jitendra Singh, who visited Wangchuk at the hospital.

According to sources, Prime Minister Modi assured that those responsible for the alleged examination irregularities would face strict and exemplary punishment. The Prime Minister also stated that the government remained committed to ensuring fairness, transparency and accountability in the examination system while protecting the interests of students.

"Just now in the presence of Union Ministers Sh. JP Nadda, Dr. Jitendra Singh and the senior leaders of Apex Body of Leh Ladakh I finally broke my fast after 26 days. Earlier 65 members of parliament from different political parties had visited or signed letters urging me to break the fast," Wangchuk said on X.

He added that the decision was taken "after long negotiations on various conditions and in view of possible violence in the country," urging supporters to remain vigilant about not allowing violence of any kind anywhere.

The 26-day fast had drawn nationwide attention, with Wangchuk demanding accountability over alleged examination irregularities and seeking assurances that no punitive action would be taken against young protesters who participated in the movement.

With Wangchuk calling off his hunger strike, attention is now expected to shift to the government's next steps on the commitments made during the discussions.
