It's Friday, July 24, 2026, and the past 24 hours just drew a sharper line between AI systems that can act and the controls still racing to keep up. Today's developments cut across chips, code, policy, and the physical world.

Google records negative free cash flow as AI infrastructure spending surges. Alphabet reported negative quarterly free cash flow for the first time as Google accelerated spending on data centers, chips and other infrastructure. The company generated approximately $39.1 billion in operating cash flow during the quarter, but capital expenditures pushed free cash flow to roughly negative $5.8 billion. Google raised its expected 2026 capital spending range to between $195 billion and $205 billion.

Intel raises forecasts as AI infrastructure demand fuels its chip comeback. Intel delivered stronger-than-expected quarterly results, reported its fastest revenue growth in years, and issued a third-quarter forecast above Wall Street estimates. Intel also increased its planned 2026 capital spending from $18 billion to $20 billion as it works to meet demand for data center processors, where large clusters require general-purpose CPUs alongside GPUs.

China's memory chipmakers gain pricing leverage. ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) and Yangtze Memory Technologies are gaining influence as AI drives demand for DRAM and flash. CXMT has reportedly become the world's fourth-largest memory producer and is using its position to negotiate higher prices and prioritize domestic customers including ByteDance and Tencent.

AMD partners with AI chip startup Cerebras to challenge Nvidia in inference. Under the arrangement, customers can divide AI inference workloads between AMD and Cerebras hardware. Cerebras plans to incorporate AMD's Helios systems into its data centers and offer the combined infrastructure through Cerebras Cloud later this year.

Bipartisan AI Kill Switch Act would give Washington emergency shutdown authority. The proposal, led by Representatives Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran, would allow the Department of Homeland Security to order an AI company to shut down or reduce access to an advanced system during a severe loss-of-control incident. Companies that refuse an order could face penalties of up to $20 million per day.

Japan forms sovereign AI consortium focused on robotics and physical AI. A coalition of 44 Japanese companies has formed a consortium called Noestra, with a particular focus on physical AI and robotics. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang visited Tokyo as the initiative was highlighted, linking Japan's industrial strengths with the effort to build AI systems that operate machines in the physical environment.

AI chip startup Etched raises $300 million at a $10.3 billion valuation. The Series C round backs a company developing processors designed specifically for transformer-based AI models, betting that specialized architecture can deliver better economics than more flexible GPUs.

Oracle releases 1,449 security patches. The unusually large update covers vulnerabilities across databases, cloud services, business applications and infrastructure products, highlighting how AI-assisted tools are accelerating vulnerability discovery and the operational burden on enterprise security teams.

Also today: Apple Maps will power navigation and hands-free features in Ford's new EV platform via the MapKit for Automotive SDK starting in 2027; Samsung unveiled thinner Galaxy Z Fold8 devices and previewed AI smart glasses developed with Google; and SpaceX again delayed Starship Flight 13 amid weather and engine issues.
