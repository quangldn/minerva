Also in today's EMEA regional roundup: SES seals strategic partnership with Elveo; French social media ban struck down; INPP cuts off funding for Toob.

Deutsche Telekom (DT) will acquire Fiberhost, a Polish fiber company that operates a network passing 1.4 million homes, from Macquarie Asset Management. It will also acquire broadband and TV retailer Inea with its 300,000 customers as part of the deal, for a joint enterprise valuation of €1 billion (US$1.16 billion). DT seeks to strengthen the transition of T-Mobila Polska from a mobile operator to a converged one through the transaction, which will have to secure the approval of Polish authorities before it can go ahead.

SES partners with Elveo Mobile

SES has announced its strategic collaboration with Elveo Mobile, the direct-do-device (D2D) operator launched today following the merger between Lynk and Omnispace. The partnership will grant SES customers access to Elveo's lower Earth orbit (LEO) network, and the companies say they will deliver an integrated multi-orbit D2D solution, while also working together on global spectrum initiatives. The Luxembourg-headquartered satellite operator has also announced it will make unspecified strategic investment in Elveo.

Ericsson hopes to hack climate change

Ericsson and Hack for Earth will host a hackathon in the UAE, inviting students, startups, innovators and other parties to explore how connectivity and digitalization can advance climate action. Participants in the Ericsson Sustainability Challenge have to be over 18 and come from the UAE, Qatar, Oman, Kuwait or Bahrain. Winning teams will join the six-month accelerator Build for Earth.

French social media ban for under-15s struck down

The French government has hit a roadblock in its race to ban under-15s from social media. The legislation has been found to breach the constitution and therefore blocked, meaning it won't come into force in September as expected. The Conseil constitutionnel – the responsible body – argued that the text of the law breached young people's rights under the constitution, even while recognising the legitimacy of protecting them from harm in some corners of the internet. While a setback, the news doesn't necessarily mark the end of the ban, with the French government set to rework it to address the problems raised by the body. The government is seeking to have it passed by spring according to French media.

INPP gives up on UK altnet Toob

UK altnet Toob has reportedly been cut off from new funding by its investor INPP, according to ISP Review, which reports that the Guernsey-based INPP has also handed off most of the £24 million (US$32.5 million) it originally invested to debt holders. Toob is active in parts of England and Scotland and has a network sharing deal with fellow altnet CityFibre. Explaining its reasoning in a note shared by the publication, INPP specifically pointed to the difficult structural environment facing UK fiber market, where consolidation has looked inevitable for quite some time now.

VMO2 renews partnership with Good Things Foundation

VMO2 will continue its work with the Good Things Foundation charity, renewing a deal that sees the operator offer free data to those in need through their National Databank. Since its launch five years ago, the initiative has connected one million people facing financial hardship and homelessness, as well as domestic abuse survivors, according to VMO2. VMO2 notes that other operators have also contributed.
