Demis Hassabis steps down as Google DeepMind CEO to become Alphabet chief scientist and focus on AGI

Source: Tech Startups — Published August 5, 2026

Demis Hassabis is stepping away from day-to-day leadership of Google DeepMind to become chairman of the AI lab and chief scientist of Alphabet, a sweeping leadership change that comes with an extraordinary explanation from the DeepMind co-founder: he believes artificial general intelligence is getting close.

"I've been working towards AGI my whole life and now, like many of you, I feel it is close at hand," Hassabis told employees Wednesday. "It's critical that we collectively get the next steps right to ensure this all goes well for humanity and we usher in an incredible new age of discovery and wonder."

The move puts Koray Kavukcuoglu, a 13-year DeepMind veteran and one of Hassabis' longtime deputies, in charge of Google DeepMind as senior vice president. Kavukcuoglu will report directly to Google and Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai and oversee Gemini model development, Frontier AI research, and the Gemini app and developer teams.

The shake-up goes beyond DeepMind. Longtime Google chief scientist Jeff Dean and Google Senior Fellow Sanjay Ghemawat are leaving to launch an independent public benefit corporation focused on machine learning, science, and engineering. Google will become a founding investor and Cloud partner in the new venture. Alphabet shares fell about 5% following the news.

Hassabis co-founded DeepMind in London in 2010 before Google acquired the company for a reported £400 million in 2014. DeepMind went on to produce AlphaGo, AlphaFold and a string of influential AI research projects. Hassabis and DeepMind researcher John Jumper shared half of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for their work on protein structure prediction.

As Alphabet chief scientist, Hassabis said he will work closely with Pichai on "strategic and global AGI matters" and continue advising DeepMind's leadership and research teams, while also spending more time at Isomorphic Labs, the Alphabet-backed drug discovery company spun out of DeepMind in 2021.

His message contained another intriguing detail: Google is already working on new models "including Gemini 4," offering a rare direct reference to the next major generation of its flagship AI system.
