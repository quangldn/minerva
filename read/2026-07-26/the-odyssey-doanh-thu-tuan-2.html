Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey is projected to have another big turnout in its second weekend at the domestic box office.

Nolan's blockbuster adaptation of Homer's epic poem earned $123.5 million in domestic ticket sales and another $140.2 million internationally for a worldwide box office tally of $263.7 million in its first weekend frame July 17-19.

Now, with no new big studio films opening this weekend and a slight bump in North American theater counts from 3,919 last week to 3,942 on Friday, Nolan's film is projected by both Deadline and The Hollywood Reporter to make $84.5 million in its second weekend frame.

If those projections hold, it will equate to a respectable 32% drop in business domestically during its second weekend from its July 17-19 opening. Box Office Pro is projecting a second weekend of anywhere from $75 million to $85 million for the Universal Pictures release, while Box Office Theory is being a bit more conservative with a $70 million to $80 million forecast.

Starring Matt Damon as Odysseus, the Greek King of Ithaca, Anne Hathaway as his wife, Queen Penelope, Tom Holland as their son, Prince Telemachus, and Zendaya as the goddess Athena, The Odyssey drew steady crowds all week. By Thursday, the film earned $199.3 million domestically and $164.1 million internationally for a running global tally of $363.6 million.

Even if The Odyssey finishes at the lower end of its projections, the film will easily blow past the $400 million mark at the global box office by Sunday, if not on Saturday.

After it was left in the wake of The Odyssey's thundering debut last weekend to finish at No. 2 domestically, Disney's live-action version of Moana is projected by Deadline to earn about $10 million in its third frame, good enough to hold onto the runner-up spot again this weekend. Disney-Pixar's Toy Story 5 is projected to earn $9.4 million for a third place finish in its sixth weekend, pushing its domestic tally to $447.9 million.

Heading into the weekend with a global box office take of $966.2 million, Toy Story 5 is within striking distance of becoming the third film to cross the $1 billion mark globally in 2026 behind The Super Mario Galaxy Movie and the Michael Jackson biopic Michael. Projected to finish fourth domestically is Minions & Monsters with $9 million, while Bleecker Street's filmed version of the Broadway sensation Hadestown: The Musical is projected to earn $7.5 million to $9 million from 1,944 venues for a fifth place opening.
