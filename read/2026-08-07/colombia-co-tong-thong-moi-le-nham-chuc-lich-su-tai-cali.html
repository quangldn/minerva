Colombia Swears In New President De la Espriella on Friday — Without Petro, and Without the Usual Ceremony

Incoming right-leaning leader takes the oath in Cali amid an unprecedented boycott by Petro, signaling a sharp foreign policy pivot and a security reset with Washington. Published Aug 05 2026, Latin Times.

CALI, Colombia — When Abelardo de la Espriella raises his right hand on Friday, Aug. 7, he will do something no elected Colombian president has done in more than a century: take the oath somewhere other than Bogotá. The venue shift is only one break with custom. Outgoing President Gustavo Petro is boycotting the event outright, a decades-old medal ceremony has quietly been dropped, and the transition has unfolded against unproven fraud claims and warnings about the country's stability.

Congress has relocated to Cali for the day, and De la Espriella will be sworn in at 3 p.m. at Arena USC, a university venue that seats roughly 2,230 people, plus an auxiliary hall for another 300. Afterward, the new president heads to a nearby army garrison for a troop review. Roughly 6,000 guests are expected, including US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Argentina's Javier Milei, Ecuador's Daniel Noboa and FIFA president Gianni Infantino.

De la Espriella first wanted to take his oath at a military garrison in Cauca department, but Petro, still commander-in-chief until the handover, refused to let any army installation host the event. Recurring eruptions from the nearby Puracé volcano kept shutting down Popayán's airport, prompting the switch to Cali. Cali has mobilized more than 11,000 security personnel, banned private drones for the day, and posted a reward of up to 500 million pesos for tips on possible threats, after authorities near Palmira arrested a suspected armed-group member carrying 20 explosive devices and a drone.

The tension traces back to a June 21 runoff that De la Espriella won by 251,854 votes, under one percentage point. Petro has refused to accept the result, alleging algorithm-driven manipulation of vote-tabulation forms, though Colombia's civil registry has rebutted his central technical claim and international observers have backed the integrity of the count. Days before the handover, Petro warned "it's clear what this could become — a civil war in Colombia," a claim neither electoral authorities nor his own government have echoed.

For Washington, the succession looks like a reset. President Trump publicly backed De la Espriella before both rounds of the vote. Colombia is set to join Washington's "Shield of the Americas" security coalition once De la Espriella takes office, with Medellín serving as the alliance's regional headquarters in the country.
