Happy Tuesday. Stocks fell Tuesday and oil prices climbed amid rising tensions between the U.S. and Iran.

President Donald Trump said he would not try to revive a stalled truce with Iran.

A memorandum of understanding the U.S. and Iran signed in June that gave the two sides a 60-day window to negotiate a lasting peace deal technically expired Monday. When Trump was asked if he would seek an extension, he replied, "No," Bloomberg reported.

Stocks closed lower Monday as oil prices rose and the 30-year Treasury yield tested multi-decade highs due to concerns about the Iran conflict.

Kyle Rodda, senior financial market analyst with Capital.com, said renewed tensions in the Middle East and subsequently higher oil prices weighed on Wall Street.

"The bellicosity was dialed up by the Trump administration to kick off the week, with US President Trump putting up a nonchalant front about the prospect of extending the existing memorandum of understanding with Iran, while threatening to bomb Oman for its negotiations with the Iranians to re-open the Strait of Hormuz," he said.

Opening Bell: Stocks Fall on War Worries, Rising Oil Prices

Stocks were falling in early trading as investors contended with fading hopes of resolving the U.S.-Iran conflict, surging oil prices and rising Treasury yields.

The S&P 500 was down 0.44%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average edged down 0.21%, and the Nasdaq lost 1.02%. The Russell 2000 gave up 0.35%.

"Markets are showing a more cautious tone today as the calm that characterized the start of the week begins to crack. U.S. equity futures are pointing lower, with the Nasdaq underperforming, as investors contend with a combination of rising long-term bond yields, renewed geopolitical uncertainty and some fresh nervousness around AI-related stocks," said Daniela Hathorn, senior market analyst at Capital.com.

Hathorn added that the bond market is arguably the bigger story, as the U.S. 10-year yield has pushed toward 4.75%, while the 30-year has climbed above 5.3%, its highest since 2007.

"This is happening despite softer recent economic data reducing expectations for an imminent Fed hike," she said. "Instead, the long end is responding to persistent inflation risks, heavy government borrowing and growing competition for capital—including debt issuance associated with the AI investment boom."

"That creates an uncomfortable environment for equities because financial conditions can tighten even without the Fed raising rates," Hathorn noted. "Geopolitics is adding to that pressure."

Market Movers: Ulta Beauty, Intuit, CoreWeave

Shares of energy infrastructure company Targa Resources (TRGP) climbed 6.7% following an announcement of 20-year, fee-based midstream agreements with ExxonMobil subsidiaries in the Permian Basin.

Ulta Beauty (ULTA) gained 5.1%. Intuit (INTU) rose 4.4% after the tax preparation software maker expanded its Intuit Intelligence suite across its mid-market products.

On the downside, cloud computing company CoreWeave (CRWV) lost 8.3% as surging long-term Treasury yields pressured the heavily leveraged artificial intelligence infrastructure company. Chipmaker Teradyne (TER) was down 8%. Lumentum Holdings (LITE) lost 7.7%.

Oil Market Update

West Texas Intermediate crude climbed 0.40% to $84.84 per barrel, while Brent crude slipped 0.18% to $90.71 per barrel.

Gold futures fell 0.47% to $4,452.70 an ounce in early trading. Silver futures fell 1.50% to $65.24 an ounce.
