Samsung is doubling down on its HBM memory and AI semiconductor ambitions, and this time, Broadcom is along for the ride in a massive way.

The two companies have signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) to deepen their partnership across memory and foundry technologies, a deal expected to be worth over $200 billion over the next five years through 2030.

The announcement came at the AI Summit in San Francisco, with Samsung's foundry chief Jinman Han and Broadcom CEO Hock Tan leading the discussions alongside senior executives and Korean government representatives.

Samsung will supply its industry-leading memory solutions, including High Bandwidth Memory (HBM), to power Broadcom's next-generation AI accelerators.

On the foundry side, Samsung will manufacture Broadcom's products, including its Wireless Broadband Communications solutions, on its 2nm and below process nodes.

The partnership also extends into advanced packaging, with 2.3D and 2.5D integration built on that same 2nm foundation, aimed at squeezing out more performance and efficiency for AI and networking chips.

Samsung's DS Division vice chairman Young Hyun Jun called AI a driver of "unprecedented demand" for integrated semiconductor tech. Broadcom's Charlie Kawwas emphasized how closely memory, foundry, and connectivity now need to work together.

With capabilities spanning memory, logic, foundry, and packaging, Samsung looks well-positioned to keep expanding its footprint in the AI infrastructure race.
