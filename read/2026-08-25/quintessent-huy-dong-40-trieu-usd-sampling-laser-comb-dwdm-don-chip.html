Quintessent, a maker of low-cost AI datacenter optical interconnect products, has raised $40 million via an oversubscribed Series A round.

Quintessent, an innovator in resilient and low-cost AI datacenter optical interconnect products, today announced it has raised $40 million in an oversubscribed Series A investment round. Quintessent also announced the start of customer sampling of its single-chip quantum dot-based DWDM comb laser – a new class of light source designed to simplify optical connectivity for rapidly scaling AI clusters.

Series A funding round is oversubscribed

The Series A funding round was led by Cycle Capital, along with new investors Goldman Sachs XIG-Industry Ventures, Hina Liberty Capital, Susquehanna International Group, InterVest, Safar Partners, and Ciena. Existing investors including Foothill Ventures, M Ventures, Osage University Partners, and Sierra Ventures also participated in the round.

Both funding and product milestones arrive at an inflection point for AI infrastructure. With the formation of the Open Compute Interconnect (OCI) MSA – backed by the industry's largest chipmakers and hyperscalers – the market has converged on wide-and-parallel dense wavelength division multiplexing (DWDM) architectures as a leading solution for optical scale-up networks, requiring many orders of magnitude more laser sources to interconnect future AI clusters. At the same time, the massive AI infrastructure buildout has collided with a worldwide shortage of Indium Phosphide (InP) lasers; a light source that almost all of today's optical interconnects universally depend on.

Quintessent addresses both realities at once:

The simplest way to light up a DWDM link: Quintessent's product generates eight precisely spaced wavelengths (lambdas) from a single laser with a single bias control. No banks of individually tuned lasers, no high-power pump lasers, nor complex wavelength-control electronics are required. The result is a laser architecture designed to reduce power consumption, simplify manufacturing, improve reliability through fewer components, and enable optical connectivity that scales for AI datacenters.

A supply chain built for the AI era: The Quintessent comb laser architecture is built with Gallium Arsenide (GaAs) O-band quantum dot gain material – a mature, high-volume material system independent of the capacity-constrained InP supply chain – that is heterogeneously integrated on standard silicon photonics to enable high-volume, wafer-scale manufacturing.
