Trump's latest 6G alliance features some big Huawei users

The NTIA, the US government agency that advises the White House on telecom policy, has issued a "call to action for 6G leadership and security," signing up 24 other countries to its latest 6G initiative. There is no overt mention of China or "high-risk vendors," but the anti-China subtext is obvious.

In a RAN market that generated $17 billion in sales for the first six months of 2026, Huawei was the second-biggest vendor outside China, ranked ahead of Nokia. Last year, Huawei earned $130.6 billion in revenues, with the $39 billion it pulled in outside China substantially more than Nokia made worldwide.

There are notable absentees: Spain, whose telcos have invested heavily in Huawei's 5G products, is not on the list, nor is India. More problematic are the Huawei users on the list — Italy and Germany. About 58% of Germany's current 5G basestations, roughly 48,000 mobile sites, came from Huawei, according to Strand Consult.

A young Nvidia tie-up with Nokia could situate the American chipmaker in the cockpit of 6G, depending on operator appetite for AI-RAN — Nvidia's term for using its GPUs in 6G networks.
