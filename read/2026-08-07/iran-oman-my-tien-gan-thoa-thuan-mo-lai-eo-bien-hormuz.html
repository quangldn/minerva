Iran, Oman, US 'close' to Hormuz deal: What do they all want?

An agreement could restore shipping through the crucial waterway, paving the way for broader peace talks between Tehran and Washington. Published 5 Aug 2026, Al Jazeera (Caolán Magee).

The United States, Iran and Oman appear to be edging closer to an interim agreement that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz, raising hopes of easing a crisis in one of the most dangerous flashpoints in the Middle East. The proposed agreement, under negotiation for several weeks, is intended to restore the June 17 ceasefire between Washington and Tehran, resolve disputes over shipping through the strategic waterway, and create space for broader negotiations on Iran's nuclear programme.

US President Donald Trump struck an optimistic tone on Tuesday evening, telling reporters negotiations with Iran were "moving along very nicely." "We'll find out. We'll know in 48 hours," he said when asked whether a deal was imminent. "The strait is going to be open very soon, or they're going to get hit very hard, and then the strait is going to be open."

Iran continues to deny holding direct talks with Washington, insisting discussions are taking place with Oman, which has long acted as mediator. Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman said the Oman talks have been "positive," although Iranian media quoted an official saying US military threats remained the main obstacle to reaching an agreement.

Commercial traffic through the waterway remains heavily disrupted — only eight vessels transited the strait on Monday, compared with roughly 130 ships per day before the US-Israel war on Iran erupted on February 28. Under a proposal reported by Axios, ships entering the Gulf would use a northern shipping lane through Iranian waters, while vessels leaving would travel through a southern lane in Omani waters coordinated with Iran, initially for 60 days with no transit fees charged.

US officials said Washington wants total freedom of commercial navigation without Iranian tolls or approval requirements. "I'm not going to let them charge," Trump said. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the Hormuz talks are separate from the wider nuclear negotiations. Experts cautioned that even a Hormuz deal would not resolve wider US-Iran disagreements or other regional flashpoints, including Israel-Hezbollah tensions in Lebanon and Houthi attacks in the Red Sea.
