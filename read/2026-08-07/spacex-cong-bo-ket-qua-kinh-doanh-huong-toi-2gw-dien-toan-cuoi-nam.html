SpaceX earnings: 2GW of compute by year end, aiming for 10-20GW by end of 2027

Source: DataCenterDynamics — Published August 5, 2026

SpaceX expects to end the year with more than 2GW of compute, CEO Elon Musk claims. In the company's first earnings report since its blockbuster IPO earlier this year, the company posted quarterly revenues of $7.8 billion, up 92 percent over last year and above analyst expectations. The company reported a net loss of $541 million, again beating expectations.

Also above expectations was AI compute infrastructure capex costs of $16bn, twice that of the previous quarter. SpaceX expects to continue high capex spending for at least two more quarters. Shares in the company fell more than 10 percent in pre-market trading.

Musk said the capex spend meant the company expected "to end this year with over 2GW of compute," up from 1.4GW of nameplate compute at the end of this quarter, 1GW in Q1, and 400MW a year ago. He added that cumulative compute online by the end of next year "may be closer to 10GW... our tentative target is to actually have 20GW at the power and cooling level online by the end of next year," though he cautioned the company likely wouldn't hit that exact number.

The company this week revealed it has begun development on a fourth data center around Memphis, Tennessee. AI compute deals remain a significant source of revenue as part of its acquisition of xAI — SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen said incremental leasing of compute capacity to companies including Anthropic, Google, and Reflection AI helped contribute $1.6bn in revenue for the company.

SpaceX plans to begin launching its 'Starmind' data center satellites next year, "which will be essentially an optimized Vera Rubin NVL72 computer," Musk said, adding that despite plans to partner with Tesla on chip development as part of Terafab, the company is currently planning to use Nvidia chips exclusively. Starlink, the low-Earth orbit internet constellation, accounted for more than half of total revenue, up 66 percent over last year, with roughly 10,200 satellites in orbit delivering about 800 terabits per second of total downlink capacity.
