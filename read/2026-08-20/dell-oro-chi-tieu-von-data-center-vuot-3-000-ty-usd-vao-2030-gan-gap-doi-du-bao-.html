Dell’Oro: Data Center Capex to Surpass $3 Trillion by 2030 as AI Buildout Accelerates

Converge Digest · 2026-08-18

Dell’Oro Group forecasts worldwide data center capex will surpass $3 trillion by 2030 as hyperscalers, neoclouds, AI model builders, and sovereign AI programs expand infrastructure investment.

Worldwide data center capital expenditures are forecast to surpass $3 trillion by 2030 as hyperscalers, AI model builders, neocloud providers, and sovereign AI programs enter another phase of infrastructure expansion, according to Dell’Oro Group. The research firm says its 2030 outlook has nearly doubled since its January 2026 forecast, reflecting higher hyperscale spending plans, increased projections for global data center power capacity, and rising commodity costs. High-end accelerators powering AI-optimized servers are expected to represent the largest component of data center capex and remain the principal spending driver through the end of the decade.

Dell’Oro also sees the composition of AI infrastructure spending broadening. General-purpose server demand is expected to benefit from inference, agentic AI, and storage workloads, adding conventional compute and memory capacity alongside accelerated systems. At the same time, Dell’Oro has added an AI-specialized cloud segment to its forecast encompassing AI model builders and neocloud service providers. The firm projects this segment to expand at nearly a 60 percent compound annual growth rate, faster than any other customer segment in its forecast. The Top 4 U.S. hyperscalers could collectively represent approximately half of worldwide data center capex, further concentrating infrastructure investment among the largest cloud operators.

The scale of the forecast also puts increasing emphasis on the physical constraints surrounding AI deployment. Dell’Oro identifies power availability, supply-chain conditions, and the sustainability of capital investment as factors that could determine the pace of expansion. Accelerated and heterogeneous computing architectures, combined with improvements in server efficiency, could partially offset rising infrastructure requirements. Enterprise investment, meanwhile, remains more constrained as organizations evaluate returns from AI deployments. Dell’Oro’s five-year forecast tracks spending across servers, storage, network infrastructure, physical infrastructure, and other data center equipment, providing a broader measure of the capital required to support the AI computing cycle.

“Our 2030 data center capex outlook has nearly doubled since the January 2026 forecast, reflecting higher hyperscale capex guidance, increased projections for global data center power capacity, and higher commodity costs,” said Baron Fung, Vice President of Research at Dell’Oro Group. “High-end accelerators powering AI-optimized servers are expected to account for the largest share of data center capex and remain the primary driver of growth over the forecast period.”

Analysis

The most significant change in Dell’Oro’s outlook is not simply the $3 trillion figure but the speed at which the forecast has been revised. In August 2025, Dell’Oro projected worldwide data center capex would reach approximately $1.2 trillion by 2029, with GPUs and custom accelerators already representing roughly one-third of total spending. The new forecast indicates that assumptions about the scale and capital intensity of AI infrastructure have shifted substantially in just a year.

The expansion also increasingly reaches beyond the accelerator itself. Large AI clusters require Scale Up connectivity between accelerators, Scale Out fabrics connecting systems across racks and clusters, and Scale Across networks connecting data centers and AI regions. That translates accelerator growth into demand for Ethernet switching, optical interconnects, coherent DCI, memory and storage, power distribution, and liquid cooling. Dell’Oro separately forecasts AI back-end switching spending to exceed $100 billion by 2030 and the data center physical infrastructure market to exceed $80 billion, illustrating how accelerator deployment propagates spending across the infrastructure stack.

The emergence of AI model builders and neoclouds as a distinct forecast category is another structural change. Dell’Oro previously grouped many of these operators within broader cloud categories. Forecasting nearly 60 percent CAGR for the new segment indicates that AI infrastructure investment is developing a substantial customer class alongside traditional hyperscalers. At the same time, Dell’Oro’s warning about power, supply chains, investment sustainability, and uncertain enterprise returns highlights the central constraint of this cycle: demand for compute can grow faster than the electrical, networking, cooling, and physical infrastructure required to deploy it.
